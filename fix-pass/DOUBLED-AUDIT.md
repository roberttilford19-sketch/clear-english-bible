# LANE D — CONSISTENCY AUDIT OF THE SIX DOUBLED "SIN AND SIN" PLACES (Ruling 6)

Report only — NO text has been changed in this lane. Each place is disposed of by the editor's ruling on this report.

The common standard applied (Ruling 6, item 5): a recorded note must establish a defensible contextual ground, not merely describe the choice already made; and (Ruling 6, item 3) the weightier keeps stand where their documented rationale demonstrates that conversion would flatten a meaningful lexical pairing — the same standard applied in both directions. The reference ground is the Exodus keep-ground: at Exodus 34:7 (twice) and 34:9 "iniquity" was KEPT because "sin" already stands beside it in the same verse (the forgiving-iniquity-transgression-and-sin formula), and at Leviticus 16:21–22 likewise in the Day of Atonement liturgy; at Exodus 20:5 it was kept because no plainer wording holds the sense without softening.

Every one of the six drafts below was verified by its own book auditor as the faithful mechanical result of the settled "iniquity" → "sin" conversion applied to a verse where the base itself pairs "iniquity" with "sin." That much is not in dispute anywhere. The audit question Ruling 6 reopens is narrower: at each place, does the repetition stand justified under the common standard, and what distinction, if any, does the conversion erase?

---

## 1. Job 10:6 — STAND (recommended)

- Source (base): "that you inquire after my iniquity, and search after my sin?"
- Draft: "that you inquire after my sin, and search after my sin?"
- Analysis: The base's pair is synonymous parallelism — two verbs (inquire / search) and two nouns aimed at one referent, Job's guilt, which Job is daring God to find. The verse's sense does not divide the guilt into two kinds; the pairing is the parallelism's variation, not a classification. The conversion erases the lexical variation and nothing else: no distinction the verse's argument depends on is lost, and the repetition itself carries the parallelism's hammering force (Job repeats himself because God, he says, keeps searching).
- Distinction erased: word-level only (the two Hebrew terms' flavors), not referential.
- Disposition recommended: the repetition is justified by the source's parallel structure; the drafting stands. The Job report's flag 4 ground, tested against the common standard, holds at this place.

## 2. Job 13:23 — STAND (recommended), flattening recorded

- Source (base): "How many are my iniquities and sins? Make me know my disobedience and my sin."
- Draft: "How many are my sins and sins? Make me know my disobedience and my sin."
- Analysis: The verse is a counting question followed by a stacked series — iniquities / sins / disobedience / sin — whose rhetoric is accumulation, not taxonomy; the second half keeps its own variation in the draft ("disobedience" renders the third term). The first clause's "sins and sins" is the least graceful of the six doublings, and this audit records that plainly; but the distinction erased is again lexical, inside a series whose other members still vary. The Exodus parity argument (see Jeremiah 36:3) reaches this place only weakly: Job's pair is a sufferer's enumeration, not a formula in which "sin" stands beside "iniquity" as a kept covenant term.
- Distinction erased: lexical variation in one member of a four-term accumulation; the verse's sense and structure survive.
- Disposition recommended: the drafting stands. If the editor ever directs relief at exactly one Job place, this is the candidate; nothing in the record requires it.

## 3. Hosea 12:8 — REOPEN, restore recommended

- Source (base): "In all my wealth they won't find in me any iniquity that is sin."
- Draft: "In all my wealth they won't find in me any sin that is sin."
- Analysis: This is the one place among the six where the two words do different grammatical work. The base's clause is a delimiting definition: Ephraim boasts that no wrongdoing will be found in his dealings *that counts as sin* — the first word names the genus (iniquity found "in me," in his wealth), the second supplies the qualifying species ("that is sin"). The converted English — "any sin that is sin" — is a tautology: the qualifier qualifies nothing, and a first-time reader cannot recover what Ephraim is claiming. The Hosea report's flag 4 note records the doubling and its mechanical justification; under the common standard that note describes the choice but does not establish a contextual ground for it, because the verse's own structure argues the other way.
- Distinction erased: a real one — the genus/species distinction inside a relative clause, on which the boast's sense depends.
- Disposition recommended: REOPEN this place for the editor's ruling, with the audit's recommendation to restore the base's word: "any iniquity that is sin." The restoration is one word, changes nothing else in the book (Hosea's other eight "iniquity" conversions stand alone and are untouched by it), and brings the verse under the same standard the Exodus keeps already satisfy.

## 4. Jeremiah 16:10 — STAND (recommended), flattening recorded

- Source (base): "‘Why has Yahweh pronounced all this great evil against us?’ or ‘What is our iniquity?’ or ‘What is our sin that we have committed against Yahweh our God?’"
- Draft: "…or ‘What is our sin?’ or ‘What is our sin that we have committed against Yahweh our God?’"
- Analysis: The people's three questions form a staircase — the evil pronounced, then iniquity, then sin *committed against Yahweh*, the third question adding the relational clause. The conversion makes the second and third questions open with the same word, flattening the staircase's middle step; but each question's full wording still differs, the escalation is carried by the added clause, and the doubled head-word is the faithful result of the conversion on a formula pair (the iniquity-and-sin question pair is a Jeremiah formula, cf. the book's own usage). No referential distinction is erased; the questions are parallel, not genus and species.
- Distinction erased: rhetorical variation between two parallel questions; sense intact.
- Disposition recommended: the drafting stands, with the flattening of the staircase recorded here as the place's cost, accepted under the common standard because the pair is formulaic parallelism (the Job 10:6 case), not a delimiting clause (the Hosea 12:8 case) or a forgiveness formula (the Jeremiah 36:3 case).

## 5. Jeremiah 36:3 — REOPEN, restore recommended

- Source (base): "It may be that the house of Judah will hear all the evil which I intend to do to them, that they may each return from his evil way; that I may forgive their iniquity and their sin."
- Draft: "…that I may forgive their sin and their sin."
- Analysis: This is the place where the packet's two lines meet head-on. The Exodus keep-ground — "iniquity" kept because "sin" stands beside it in the same verse, in a divine forgiveness pronouncement (Exodus 34:7, 34:9) and in the atonement liturgy (Leviticus 16:21–22) — describes Jeremiah 36:3 exactly: a divine forgiveness pronouncement ("that I may forgive…") in which "iniquity" and "sin" stand paired as the joint object of forgiving. Ruling 6 adopted one common standard for both directions: keeps stand where conversion would flatten a meaningful lexical pairing. Applied evenly, that standard reaches this conversion: the pairing here is the same pairing Exodus kept for, in the same speech register (God declaring what he will forgive), and the Jeremiah record supplies no ground that distinguishes this verse from the Exodus configuration — its counting note states only that the conversion "stands beside the base's own 'sin' in the same verse," which is the keep-ground stated from the other side. Under Ruling 6 item 5, that describes the choice; it does not establish a ground for it.
- Distinction erased: the forgiveness formula's paired object — the full breadth of what is forgiven, named in two words as at Exodus 34.
- Disposition recommended: REOPEN this place for the editor's ruling, with the audit's recommendation to restore the base's word: "that I may forgive their iniquity and their sin." If the editor instead rules that the Exodus ground is local to the Decalogue and Day of Atonement contexts, that distinction is his to make and will be recorded as the ground this place then stands on; it is not in the present record and this audit does not invent it.

## 6. Nehemiah 9:2 — STAND (recommended)

- Source (base): "The offspring of Israel separated themselves from all foreigners and stood and confessed their sins and the iniquities of their fathers."
- Draft: "…stood and confessed their sins and the sins of their fathers."
- Analysis: The pair's two members have different possessors — the present generation's sins, the fathers' iniquities — inside the great Levitical confession, where the distinction the sentence actually draws is between generations, and that distinction survives conversion intact ("their sins and the sins of their fathers" loses no information a reader needs). The base's word-change is variation across the pair, not a classification. This is the least costly of the six doublings.
- Distinction erased: word-level variation only; the generational contrast, which is the verse's point, is untouched.
- Disposition recommended: the repetition is justified; the drafting stands. The Nehemiah report's flag 7 disclosure, tested against the common standard, holds at this place.
