## Judith 7:27 — "our souls will live"

The verse (as recorded): "For we will be servants, and our souls will live, and we will not see the death of our babies before our eyes, and our wives and our children fainting in death." The speakers are the besieged men of Bethulia, choosing capture and slavery over watching their families die.

**The ground.** The keep stands on two limbs, both verse-specific:

1. *A life-outcome is predicated of the souls as subject.* "Our souls will live" predicates living of the souls directly, and the verse sets the counter-term in the same breath — the death of the babies, wives and children fainting in death. Survival-against-death predicated of the soul is the Trigger 1 pattern (Ruling 3): an outcome (living, deliverance from the death that fills the rest of the verse) is predicated of the soul itself, not merely recorded of the persons. No count is taken anywhere in the verse — no numeral, no tally gloss, no located company — so Trigger 2's factual condition is absent, not merely outweighed.
2. *The soul here is the speakers' own selves, and no token swap preserves the sense.* Audit Crew BC-1's best reading stands on the record: the subject is the speakers themselves — "we ourselves will live." "People" would name the nation, not the speakers; "we will live" would delete the subject the verse chose. BC-1 placed the verse in the Numbers 31:50 "ourselves" family of soul questions, and Numbers 31:50 is itself a confirmed reserved keep under Ruling 4.

**How this ground can fail (the standing challenge).** BC-1 ruled the verse a *genuine boundary case*, and that ruling is the challenge this ground must survive: the countable-person branch is genuinely arguable, because "our souls will live" can be read as a survival periphrasis — the persons live — with the soul as a mere self-reference rather than the subject of a predicated outcome. If the editor judges limb 1 to be periphrasis rather than predication, the ground collapses to limb 2 alone (no count taken; no swap preserves sense), which under the falsifiability guard is not sufficient by itself. In that event Ruling 5 directs the outcome: the place is REOPENED for a ruling — not converted by this desk.

**Drafting verdict.** The record supports a demonstrated, challengeable ground, stated above. It is the weaker of this lane's two grounds, and it stands exactly as strong as limb 1: on the predication reading, the keep is a Trigger 1 keep of the same shape as the confirmed family; on the periphrasis reading, the place reopens. No reopening is recommended on the present record, because BC-1's own best reading ("we ourselves will live," subject = the speakers themselves, survival predicated against the verse's deaths) sustains limb 1 — but the ground is recorded as challengeable in precisely that term, so a future challenge knows where to strike.

## Revelation 18:13 — "people's bodies and souls"

The verse (as recorded): the merchandise list of Babylon's traders ends "…and people's bodies and souls." The word stands paired with "bodies," in the base's own pairing, at the end of the cargo list (Revelation 18:12–13).

**The ground.** The keep stands on two limbs, both verse-specific:

1. *The instance falls outside every converted precedent's scope, on the precedents' own terms.* The audited counting conversions — the places Trigger 2 (Ruling 3) rests on — convert "souls" standing alone under a number: Acts 2:41 ("about three thousand souls"), Acts 7:14 ("seventy-five souls"), Acts 27:37 (the ship's company under its number), and the Genesis 46 counts Robert confirmed at Ruling 16. This instance is *paired and unnumbered*: no numeral attaches to it, no tally is taken, and the word does not stand alone — it stands as the second member of "bodies and souls." As REV-AUD-B's concurrence states it, the counting precedents were verified on the records to convert only souls standing alone under a number, which this instance is not. Trigger 2's factual condition (souls merely tallied as a headcount of persons) is therefore absent.
2. *The pairing does work a conversion would silently decide.* "Bodies and souls" names the whole human being as the traders' merchandise — the horror of the list's climax is that persons are cargo in both members, body and soul together. Converting the second member ("people's bodies and people") would collapse the base's merism, duplicate "people" around "bodies," and silently decide the body/soul composition the base states — exactly what Ruling 5's standing caveat forbids treating as settled by a conversion.

**How this ground can fail (the standing challenge).** A cargo list is a stock inventory in spirit, and the challenge writes itself: if "bodies and souls" is read as an ornate tally of enslaved persons — souls functioning as the count of the traded — the instance approaches the counting branch, and limb 1's "unnumbered" distinction becomes a technicality rather than a difference of function. The ground fails if that reading is sustained against the pairing's merism function in limb 2. The drafting records the challenge at that strength: the ground's answer is that every converted precedent carries an actual number and an unpaired word, and that the burden under Ruling 6's common standard is a contextual ground, which the pairing supplies — but a challenger who shows the pair functioning as a pure tally in this list defeats it, and the place then reopens under Ruling 5.

**Drafting verdict.** The record supports a demonstrated, challengeable ground, stated above; no reopening is recommended on the present record.
