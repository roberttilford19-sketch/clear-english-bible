# LANE G JUDGMENT LOG — soul candidates under the two-trigger rule (Ruling 11; rule text at Ruling 3)

Method: each place judged trigger by trigger against its staged text and its book's audit record. Trigger 1: an outcome (saving, destruction, death, deliverance, or the like) predicated of the soul itself → "soul" stands. Trigger 2: souls merely tallied as a headcount of persons → "people." Outcome of this lane: **no text changes** — no candidate place is a headcount tally, so Trigger 2 fires nowhere; every place either settles under Trigger 1, stands with the rule not engaged (classification recorded), or is held. Each judgment below names its trigger and ground.

## Settled under Trigger 1 — "soul" stands

- **Ezekiel 3:19** — Trigger 1. Ground: deliverance is predicated of the watchman's soul ("you have delivered your soul"); EI-1's candidacy ground (conditional, prospective, reflexive self-deliverance) explains the candidacy but does not remove the predication, and the settled-out class at Isaiah 38:17 requires both object-position and a completed retrospective deliverance — the second leg fails here, as EI-1 itself ruled. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 3:21** — Trigger 1. Same construction and ground as 3:19. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 3:19 / 3:21 note** — Ruling 11 carried EI-1's ground on the record; it is carried here too: the candidacy was honest, and the rule as adopted judges by predication, which is present.
- **Ezekiel 13:18, candidate occurrences (×2: "to hunt souls"; "Will you hunt the souls of my people")** — Trigger 1. Ground: the souls are the objects of a life-and-death practice whose stated outcomes attach to the souls themselves in the same oracle — 13:19 (member) "kill the souls who should not die… save the souls alive who should not live"; and EJ-1 considered the counting/persons (branch-1) shape at these very verses and ruled it not governing. The verse's member occurrence ("save souls alive for yourselves") stands under Ruling 4. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 13:20, candidate occurrences (×2: "you hunt the souls to make them fly"; "the souls whom you ensnare like birds")** — Trigger 1. Ground: as at 13:18 — ensnaring and flight are fates predicated of the souls inside the same oracle whose member occurrence is deliverance ("I will let the souls fly free," Ruling 4), and EJ-1 ruled the branch-1 shape not governing at this verse. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 14:14** — Trigger 1. Ground: deliverance predicated of the souls ("they would deliver only their own souls by their righteousness"), on EI-1's governing ground as at 3:19. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 14:20** — Trigger 1. Same construction and ground as 14:14. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 18:4** — Trigger 1. Ground: the verse's governing clause predicates death of the soul itself ("The soul who sins, he will die"); the belonging occurrences ("all souls are mine; as the soul of the father, so also the soul of the son is mine") ride inside the verse whose stated principle is that death-predication, and EK-1's resumptive-pronoun question is answered by the clause itself, which predicates dying of the soul as subject-term. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 18:20** — Trigger 1. Ground: "The soul who sins, he will die" — death predicated of the soul itself, the cleanest form of the trigger in the chapter. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 18:27** — Trigger 1. Ground: saving predicated of the soul ("he will save his soul alive"), the reflexive-save shape EK-1 recorded; as at 3:19, reflexivity does not remove the predication. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 33:5** — Trigger 1. Ground: deliverance predicated of the soul ("he would have delivered his soul"), on EI-1's ground via EM-1. Stands.
- **Ezekiel 33:9** — Trigger 1. Ground: the watchman recurrence of 3:19's wording; same ground. Stands.
- **Sirach 6:2, second occurrence** — Trigger 1. Ground: destruction is predicated directly of the soul ("that your soul not be torn in pieces like a bull") — Audit Crew DK-1's own candidacy ground is the trigger's text, and the place is the mirror of Wisdom 1:11, a confirmed Ruling 4 member. The verse's first occurrence stands as an inner-self keep, the split treatment ruled sound by DK-1. Stands.
- **Matthew 10:28 (both instances)** — Trigger 1. Ground: killing and destruction are predicated of the soul in both clauses ("not able to kill the soul"; "able to destroy both soul and body in Gehenna"). Stands.
- **Lamentations 2:12** — Trigger 1. Ground: the soul's pouring-out is the children's dying, in the wounded-in-the-streets frame ("when they swoon as the wounded… when their soul is poured out into their mothers' bosom") — the same figure as Isaiah 53:12 ("he poured out his soul to death"), a confirmed Ruling 4 member. Distinguished from Isaiah 58:10's settled-out pour-out on that record's own ground: there the pour-out is the giver's act of self-giving, not an outcome befalling the soul; here it befalls the children as they die. Stands.

## Standing with the rule not engaged — classification recorded, no conversion arguable

- **Isaiah 3:9** — Stands as an inner-self keep. Ground: on the book's own record (DR-1), the disaster attaches to "themselves," the soul standing as inner self by metonymy — so Trigger 1 is not cleanly engaged, and Trigger 2 (no tally anywhere in the verse) cannot fire. Nothing in the rule or the record supports "people" ("Woe to their people" is a different sentence). Classification recorded for the editor's read-through.
- **Matthew 26:38** — Stands as an inner-self keep. Ground: what is predicated of the soul is sorrow ("My soul is exceedingly sorrowful"); "even to death" measures the sorrow's depth and does not predicate death of the soul, so Trigger 1's outcome family is not engaged; no tally, so Trigger 2 cannot fire. Classification recorded.
- **Mark 14:34** — Stands. Same words and same ground as Matthew 26:38.
- **Hebrews 4:12** — Stands as a constituent keep. Ground: the soul is named as a constituent of the person being distinguished ("dividing of soul and spirit"); division is not in Trigger 1's outcome family (saving, destruction, death, deliverance), and no tally engages Trigger 2. Classification recorded.
- **Hebrews 6:19** — Stands. Ground: the clause's predicates ("sure and steadfast") attach to the hope, which is figured as the soul's anchor; no outcome in the trigger's family is predicated of the soul itself, and no tally engages Trigger 2. Classification recorded — the anchoring figure is the weakest form of engagement in this lane, and the editor's eye may revisit the classification at read-through; the text stands under any reading of it.
- **Hebrews 10:38** — Stands as an inner-self keep (the divine self). Ground: the soul here is the subject who feels ("my soul has no pleasure in him"), not the object of an outcome; neither trigger engages. Noted beside its neighbor: 10:39 is the confirmed Trigger 1 member ("the saving of the soul"), so the pair discriminates within two verses exactly as the rule intends.

## HELD for the editor — one place

- **Leviticus 22:3** — HELD, text untouched. The two sides, both on the record: (a) For Trigger 1: being "cut off from before me" is an outcome — the excision of the person from the covenant presence — predicated of "that soul" in the verse's own words, which is the trigger's shape. (b) Against: the audited precedent line for this exact construction runs the other way — Exodus 12:15 and 12:19 and Leviticus 7:20, 21, 25, 27 rendered cut-off "soul" instances as "person," Leviticus 18:29 as "people," and only Leviticus 17:10 kept, in its blood-and-life context — and the Leviticus auditor concurred in this keep while expressly dissenting from its reasoning on exactly that ground, a dissent Ruling 11 carried onto the record rather than erasing. Judgment: the rule's letter and the settled construction-line conflict at this verse, and either disposition silently overrules one side of a record the editor ordered preserved. Held for the editor's disposition, with both sides stated. The verse stands in the base's word meanwhile, as it has throughout.
