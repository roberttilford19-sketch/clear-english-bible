# TORAH AUDIT — the Clear English Bible's five books of Moses at seven named seats

**DRAFT — desk audit, prepared overnight 2026-11-19/20 under Robert Tilford's order of 2026-11-19 23:10, the Torah audit being the named priority of his standing shelf-audit charge of 2026-11-19. Nothing in this report has been published, posted, sent, or pushed; it is a draft for Robert's eyeball.**

**What this audit is, stated in the same breath as its findings:** this is a desk audit. The desk is not a credentialed Hebraist, and this report does not present itself as one. What the desk did is mechanical and checkable: it read the Clear English Bible's text at each named seat from the landed reader files under `~/workspace/ceb-push/individual-books/`, set it beside the published Masoretic wording of the same seat, and set both beside an independent scholarly translation as witness. The Masoretic text and the witness translation were fetched on 2026-11-20 from Sefaria's library, which serves the Masoretic text and the Revised JPS translation (RJPS, *The JPS Tanakh: Gender-Sensitive Edition*). Transliterations are the desk's own, in a plain academic style. Under Robert's Tanakh source standard of 2026-11-18, the Masoretic text is the source of record and the Septuagint a witness; the Septuagint was not separately checked at these seats tonight, and that limit is stated here rather than discovered later.

**The headline, with its scope in the same breath:** at all seven seats the Clear English Bible's text stands faithful to the Masoretic wording or renders it defensibly, and at no seat was the text smoothed, harmonised, or made to say something the Hebrew does not say. Five seats grade **faithful**, one grades **faithful in a defensible rendering**, and one — the *elohim* seats of Exodus 21–22 — grades **a defensible rendering with a signpost gap**: the text follows the Masoretic word and the scholarly witness's own main text, but the reader is nowhere told that a recognised second reading exists. That gap is the same shape as the Hebrews 8:9 finding of 2026-11-19, and it is the only systemic finding of this audit. Each grade below carries its evidence in the same breath.

## Seat 1 — Genesis 1:26. Grade: FAITHFUL.

The Clear English Bible reads: "God said, 'Let us make man in our image, like us. Let them rule over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the sky, over the tame animals, over all the earth, and over every creature that crawls on the earth.'" (GENESIS-reader.txt, Genesis 1.)

The Masoretic wording is *vayyo'mer Elohim: na'aseh adam betzalmenu kidmutenu, veyirdu…* — "And God said: Let us make humankind in our image, after our likeness, and let them rule…" The plural is in the Hebrew itself: *na'aseh* is a plural verb ("let us make"), *betzalmenu* carries a plural suffix ("in our image"), and the ruling verb that follows is plural ("let them rule"). The witness agrees: RJPS reads, "And God said, 'Let us make humankind in our image, after our likeness. They shall rule the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, the cattle, the whole earth, and all the creeping things that creep on earth.'"

The seat matters because a translation under pressure could have singularised the verse. This one did not. The plural stands in the CEB exactly where the Masoretic text puts it, and the verse that follows (1:27) keeps the Hebrew's own singular — "God made man in his own image" for *vayyivra Elohim et-ha'adam betzalmo* — so the alternation a reader meets in the CEB is the alternation that stands in the Hebrew, not an editor's hand.

## Seat 2 — Genesis 19:24. Grade: FAITHFUL.

The Clear English Bible reads: "Then Yahweh rained on Sodom and on Gomorrah sulfur and fire from Yahweh out of the sky." (GENESIS-reader.txt, Genesis 19.)

The Masoretic wording is *vaYHWH himtir 'al-Sedom ve'al-'Amorah gafrit va'esh me'et YHWH min-hashamayim* — "And YHWH rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire **from YHWH** out of heaven." The Name occurs twice in one verse, once as the subject who rains and once as the source from whom the fire comes, and the verse has been a proof-text and a puzzle for exactly that reason. The witness keeps both occurrences: RJPS reads, "GOD rained upon Sodom and Gomorrah sulfurous fire **from GOD** out of heaven" — GOD being RJPS's rendering of the Tetragrammaton.

The CEB likewise keeps both. It did not collapse the second "Yahweh" into "himself," a pronoun, or silence, and it did not import any agent the Hebrew does not name. Whatever a reader makes of the doubled Name, he is reading what the Masoretic text actually says. The only modernisation at the seat is "sulfur" for the base text's "brimstone," which is a vocabulary update, not a change of sense: *gafrit* is sulfur.

## Seat 3 — Exodus 7:1. Grade: FAITHFUL, in a defensible rendering.

The Clear English Bible reads: "Yahweh said to Moses, 'Look, I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother will be your prophet.'" (EXODUS-reader.txt, Exodus 7.)

The Masoretic wording is *re'eh netattikha Elohim le-Phar'oh, ve'Aharon achikha yihyeh nevi'ekha* — "See, I have given you (as) *elohim* to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet." The Hebrew has no word for "as": it says, starkly, "I have made you *elohim* to Pharaoh," and every English rendering must decide how to carry a man being set in the *elohim* role toward another man. The witness decides it the same way the CEB does: RJPS reads, "See, I place you **in the role of God** to Pharaoh, with your brother Aaron as your prophet." The CEB's "as God" and RJPS's "in the role of God" are the same decision in different words.

The seat's paired structure also stands intact in the CEB: Moses in God's role, Aaron as his *navi*, his prophet — the spokesman who speaks for the one he serves, exactly as the Hebrew pairs them and as Exodus 4:16 sets the same pattern ("he will speak for you to the people… and you will be as God to him," a seat outside this audit's list, named here only as the pattern's other occurrence). Nothing at this seat was diluted.

## Seat 4 — Exodus 21–22, the *elohim* seats. Grade: DEFENSIBLE RENDERING, with a signpost gap.

This is the audit's one seat with a finding, and the finding is about the signpost, not the text.

The Hebrew word at every one of these seats is *elohim*. At Exodus 21:6 the Masoretic wording is *vehiggisho adonav el-ha'elohim* — "his master shall bring him to *ha-Elohim*" — and the CEB reads, "then his master must bring him **to God**, and must bring him to the door or to the doorpost." At Exodus 22:8 in English numbering (22:7 in the Masoretic numbering) the wording is *veniqrav ba'al-habbayit el-ha'elohim* — "the master of the house shall come near to *ha-Elohim*" — and the CEB reads, "then the master of the house must come near **to God**." At 22:9 (Masoretic 22:8), *'ad ha'elohim yavo devar-shenehem; asher yarshi'un Elohim* — "the case of both shall come to *ha-Elohim*; the one whom *Elohim* condemns" — and the CEB reads, "the case of both parties must come **before God**. He whom **God** condemns must pay double." At 22:28 (Masoretic 22:27), *Elohim lo teqallel* — "You shall not revile *Elohim*" — and the CEB reads, "You must not speak evil against **God**, nor curse a ruler of your people."

At every seat the scholarly witness's main text makes the same choice the CEB makes. RJPS reads "his master shall take him **before God**" at 21:6, "the owner of the house shall depose **before God**" at 22:8, "the case of both parties shall come **before God**: the one whom **God** declares guilty" at 22:9, and "You shall not **revile God**" at 22:28. The CEB's renderings are inherited from its World English Bible base, and they stand inside the scholarly mainstream, not outside it. A control observation confirms that the pattern is fidelity to the base's word-for-word habit rather than an avoidance of the word "judges": at Exodus 21:22, where the Hebrew word is not *elohim* but *pelilim* — the arbiters — the CEB does print "the judges": "he will surely be fined as much as the woman's husband demands and **the judges** allow." Where the Hebrew says judges, the CEB says judges; where the Hebrew says *elohim*, the CEB says God.

The dissent, stated fairly in the same breath: there is a second, older reading of these seats, and it is not a fringe one. The Aramaic Targum Onkelos renders *elohim* at these legal seats as *dayyana*, the judges; Rashi glosses *el-ha'elohim* at 21:6 as "to the judges"; and the King James Version prints "the judges" in its text at 21:6 and 22:8–9. RJPS itself acknowledges the reading in a footnote at 21:6 — "before God: **in contrast to others, 'to the judges'**" — and cross-refers the same note at 22:8. On this reading, the slave's ear, the householder's oath, and the disputed property are brought before a human court acting in God's name, and the verses regulate court procedure rather than a sanctuary rite. The desk offers no ruling between the readings: "God" follows the Masoretic word's plain sense and the modern scholarly main text; "judges" follows the oldest continuous interpretive tradition of the passage. Both are defensible. What is not defensible is the reader never learning that the choice exists.

That is the gap. The Torah reader files carry no footnotes or apparatus anywhere (a mechanical count of the Genesis, Exodus, and Deuteronomy reader files finds zero notes), so at the one set of seats in this audit where a recognised second reading changes who is standing in front of the slave and the householder, the CEB prints its reading in silence. This is the Hebrews 8:9 pattern of 2026-11-19 repeating on the Torah side: the base text carried a faithful rendering, and the missing piece is the signpost, not the text. A proposed note is offered at the end of this report for Robert's ruling; nothing has been executed.

## Seat 5 — Exodus 23:20–21. Grade: FAITHFUL.

The Clear English Bible reads: "'Look, I send an angel before you, to guard you on the way, and to bring you into the place which I have prepared. Pay attention to him, and listen to his voice. Don't provoke him, for he will not forgive your disobedience, **for my name is in him.**'" (EXODUS-reader.txt, Exodus 23.)

The Masoretic wording is *hinneh anokhi sholeach mal'akh lefanekha… ki shemi beqirbo* — "Behold, I send a *mal'akh* before you… for **My name is in him**." The clause the seat turns on, *ki shemi beqirbo*, stands in the CEB word for word: "for my name is in him." The witness reads the same: RJPS, "I am sending an angel before you to guard you on the way… since **My Name is in him**." The CEB also keeps the hard edge of the verse — the messenger who bears the Name "will not forgive your disobedience," for *lo yissa lefishe'khem*, "he will not pardon your offenses" in RJPS — where a softer translation might have blunted a subordinate figure's refusal to pardon.

One small signpost note, of a lower order than Seat 4's: *mal'akh* ranges across "angel" and "messenger," and RJPS's own footnote at verse 20 records a named traditional reading — "Or 'a messenger,' namely Moses; cf. Gersonides, Luzzatto." The CEB's "angel" matches RJPS's main text and the plain sense of a figure who carries the Name and must be obeyed as God is obeyed (verse 22: "if you indeed listen to his voice, and do all that I speak"). This is a lexical range, not a textual divergence, and the desk records it for completeness rather than as a defect.

## Seat 6 — Deuteronomy 18:15–22. Grade: FAITHFUL.

The Clear English Bible reads, at verse 15: "Yahweh your God will raise up to you a prophet from among you, of your brothers, like me. You must listen to him." At verse 18: "I will raise them up a prophet from among their brothers, like you. I will put my words in his mouth, and he will speak to them all that I will command him." And the passage's two tests stand complete: verse 20, "But the prophet who speaks a word presumptuously in my name, which I have not commanded him to speak, or who speaks in the name of other gods, that same prophet will die"; and verse 22, "When a prophet speaks in Yahweh's name, if the thing doesn't follow, nor happen, that is the thing which Yahweh has not spoken. The prophet has spoken it presumptuously. You must not be afraid of him." (DEUTERONOMY-reader.txt, Deuteronomy 18.)

The Masoretic wording at verse 15 is *navi miqqirbekha me'achekha kamoni yaqim lekha YHWH Elohekha* — "A prophet from your midst, from your brothers, like me, YHWH your God will raise up for you" — and at verse 18, *navi aqim lahem miqqerev achehem kamokha, venatatti devarai befiv* — "A prophet I will raise up for them from among their brothers, like you, and I will put My words in his mouth." *Navi* is singular in both verses, *kamoni/kamokha* is "like me / like you," and the CEB preserves all three facts without addition or subtraction: the prophet is one, he comes from among the brothers, and he is like Moses. The witness agrees throughout: RJPS reads "a prophet like myself" at verse 15 and "a prophet like yourself, in whose mouth I will put My words" at verse 18. Nothing in the passage was pluralised into a line of prophets, nothing was singularised out of a collective, and neither test was dropped or softened — the death sentence for the presumptuous prophet and the non-fulfilment test both stand as written.

## Seat 7 — Deuteronomy 34:10. Grade: FAITHFUL.

The Clear English Bible reads: "Since then, there has not arisen a prophet in Israel like Moses, whom Yahweh knew face to face." (DEUTERONOMY-reader.txt, Deuteronomy 34.)

The Masoretic wording is *velo-qam navi 'od beYisra'el keMosheh, asher yeda'o YHWH panim el-panim* — "And there has not arisen again in Israel a prophet like Moses, whom YHWH **knew**, face to face." The CEB's "whom Yahweh knew face to face" is the literal line, and at this word it stands closer to the Hebrew than the witness does: RJPS renders *yeda'o* idiomatically as "whom GOD **singled out**, face to face," a defensible reading of *yada'* in its sense of choosing, but a step away from the word on the page. The CEB, following its base, kept the word on the page. The verse's claim — no prophet like Moses has arisen since, measured by that face-to-face knowledge — stands in the CEB exactly as the Masoretic text states it, neither heightened nor hedged.

## The count, reconciled

Seven seats were named in the commission and seven seats are reported above: Genesis 1:26; Genesis 19:24; Exodus 7:1; Exodus 21–22 (four *elohim* occurrences audited: 21:6; 22:8; 22:9; 22:28 in English numbering, with the Masoretic numbering given at each); Exodus 23:20–21; Deuteronomy 18:15–22 (all eight verses read, the load-bearing verses quoted); Deuteronomy 34:10. Grades: **faithful — five seats** (Genesis 1:26; Genesis 19:24; Exodus 23:20–21; Deuteronomy 18:15–22; Deuteronomy 34:10); **faithful in a defensible rendering — one seat** (Exodus 7:1); **defensible rendering with a signpost gap — one seat** (Exodus 21–22). **Divergences from the Masoretic wording: none found.** No seat was harmonised toward another book, no seat was smoothed, and the two verses in this list most exposed to theological pressure in either direction — the doubled Name at Genesis 19:24 and the singular prophet at Deuteronomy 18 — both stand as the Hebrew stands.

## Contradictions and dissent, kept on the record

One genuine scholarly disagreement touches this audit and is preserved rather than resolved: at the Exodus 21–22 seats, the modern scholarly main text (RJPS, and the CEB by inheritance) reads *elohim* as "God," while the Targumic and rabbinic tradition, and the King James text, read "judges." RJPS's own footnote at 21:6 is the audit's fetched evidence that the second reading is a recognised scholarly alternative and not a sectarian invention. The desk's grade of "defensible" applies to the CEB's choice precisely because the disagreement is real; had either reading been the only one the tradition knows, the grade would have been "faithful" or "divergence" accordingly. A lesser disagreement of the same kind stands at Exodus 23:20, where *mal'akh* is "angel" in both main texts and "a messenger, namely Moses" in a named traditional reading RJPS footnotes. No other contradiction was found: at no seat did the witness's main text and the CEB part company on a matter of substance, and at Deuteronomy 34:10 the CEB is the more literal of the two.

## What this audit did not do

It did not audit the Septuagint at these seats, did not audit any seat outside the seven named, and did not re-translate anything. It graded the CEB's printed text against the Masoretic wording and one scholarly witness, RJPS; a second witness (the desk notes Everett Fox's Schocken translation is carried in the same library) was available but not needed, because at no seat did the first witness and the CEB disagree in a way a second witness would have arbitrated. If Robert wants the elohim seats carried to a second and third witness — Fox, Alter, and the Targum in its own text — that is a bounded follow-up this report names but does not perform.

## Recommendation, for Robert's ruling — nothing executed

On the pattern Robert set at Hebrews 8:9 on 2026-11-19 — each text printed as it stands, the divergence signposted in plain words — the desk recommends one note, placed at Exodus 21:6 where the seat first occurs, in the same plain register as the proposed Hebrews note:

> "Note: The Hebrew word here is *elohim*. It is printed 'God,' as the word stands; the old Jewish interpreters and the King James Bible read it here as 'the judges' — the court acting in God's name. The same word stands at 22:8, 22:9, and 22:28."

Whether the note is added, and whether the Exodus 23:20 messenger reading earns a shorter one, is Robert's ruling to make. The texts themselves need no correction at any of the seven seats, and this audit recommends none.
