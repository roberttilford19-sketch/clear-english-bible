## FLAGS CARRIED FOR ROBERT

1. **The deliberative-question item, as it touches this book — PENDING ROBERT, with this book's places at 2:1 (twice), 5:19, 11:11, 16:20, 17:6, and 19:22.** In each place a speaker weighs, in question form, an act that lies in his own power or in the power of the body he leads, and asks what ought to be done; the lane has not settled one uniform treatment for that class, and no verse in this book changes until Robert rules. At 2:1 David inquires of Yahweh twice — “Will I go up into any of the cities of Judah?” and “Where will I go up?” — both rendered “will” as drafted and confirmed genuine deliberatives by Audit Crew AA-1. At 5:19 David inquires of Yahweh, “Will I go up against the Philistines?”, likewise rendered “will” and confirmed by AA-1; the “must” in the same block is a different matter, the prohibition at 5:23 (“You must not go up”), which prescribes the circling maneuver and is not a question. At 11:11 Uriah asks, “Shall I then go into my house to eat and to drink, and to lie with my wife?” — and there the base's “Shall” itself stands retained in the verse, unchanged, confirmed by Audit Crew AB-1 as a genuine deliberative question and recorded as an added place on the item. That retained “Shall” at 11:11 is the book's only remaining “shall”: the base's other 27 “shall” occurrences in this book were converted, 23 to “will” and 4 to “must,” and the reader's single “shall” is this disclosed keeping, not an oversight and not an assembly slip. At 16:20 Absalom asks his council, “Give your counsel what we will do,” and at 17:6 he asks Hushai, “Will we do what he says?” — both rendered “will” as drafted and verified genuine deliberatives by Audit Crew AC-1, counsel-seeking questions on what ought to be done. At 19:22 David asks, “Will any man be put to death today in Israel?” Audit Crew AD-1 placed this verse on the item, and did not sustain the drafter's classification of it as a possibility/fact question: David is the decision-maker, weighing in rhetorical-question form an act that lies in his own power, in answer to Abishai's proposal at verse 21, and his decree follows at verse 23 in the base's own “will” (“You will not die”), which is not itself a “shall” place. The reasoning stands in full in chapter 19's audit record. Three further rulings from this book's audits bound the item, and they are recorded here so a later ruling is not read more widely than the audits placed it. At 2:26 Abner's “Will the sword devour forever?” was concurred in by AA-1 as a possibility/fact question, not a deliberative: it protests an outcome, it does not seek counsel on the speaker's own act. At 24:13 Gad's choice questions were sustained by AD-1 in the fact family, with the distinguishing reasoning stated in chapter 24's audit record: Gad enumerates offered outcomes — famine, flight, and pestilence — that are not David's acts, and David's own deliberation follows separately at verse 14, which contains no “shall.” At 10:11 Joab's “then you will help me” was placed by AB-1 on the conditional-outcome line, expressly not in the Numbers arrangement family: the “shall” there states what will happen under a stated battlefield condition, reciprocally between the two commanders, in a sentence whose parallel limb the base itself phrases with its own “will” — so a later ruling on the Numbers arrangement material does not sweep this verse. One declarative member of the same family stands in the neighboring book and is named in chapter 19's audit record: 1 Samuel 11:13, a stated refusal in declarative form, placed by its own audit as the family's declarative member, of which 19:22 is the interrogative member. Finally, the base's own “should” stands unchanged in this book at three places named in the chapter records as data points for the same question, none of them a conversion: 3:33 (“Should Abner die as a fool dies?”), 7:5 (“Should you build me a house for me to dwell in?”), and 21:3 (“What should I do for you? And with what should I make atonement”).

2. **The “soul” line, with this book's five instances — all audit-accepted keeps, and no countable-person conversion in this book.** The line the audits apply is this: convert “soul” only where “soul” names a countable person; keep “soul” where it names the inner self or life, desire, or the self spoken of in an oath. Compiled from this book's chapter audit records, every “soul” instance in 2 Samuel is a keep, and no chapter record in this book carries a countable-person conversion of “soul” to “person” or “people.” At 3:21 Abner says David may reign “over all that your soul desires” — “soul” names the inner self and its desire, and Audit Crew AA-1 concurred in the keeping. At 4:9 David swears, “As Yahweh lives, who has redeemed my soul out of all adversity” — “soul” names his own self and life, and AA-1 concurred. At 5:8 the Jebusites are “hated by David's soul” — “soul” names David's inner self, and AA-1 concurred. At 11:11 Uriah's oath, “As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing!”, keeps “soul” as the life and self spoken of in an oath formula, and Audit Crew AB-1 ruled it a correct keep under the same branch. At 14:19 the woman's oath, “As your soul lives, my lord the king,” is the same oath construction, and Audit Crew AC-1 concurred in the keeping. Chapters 19–24 contain no “soul” instance at all: Audit Crew AD-1 verified the count of zero on the whole snapshot files, not on the verse sections alone, so no instance hides in a snapshot's apparatus or footnote line. The line itself rides for Robert's read-through, and any of the five keepings is his to overrule there.
