## FLAGS CARRIED FOR ROBERT

1. **HEADLINE — The arrangement / standing-duty “shall” item, PENDING ROBERT.** In chapter 2, everything after verse 2's opening command took “will”: who will encamp on each side (vv. 3–31), the prince who “will be” each tribe's prince, the standard that “will be” on each side, and the order in which the camps “will set out,” first, second, third, and last — 26 “will” renderings in that chapter after the two “must” renderings at verse 2. In chapter 3, the duty and arrangement statements of verses 23–38 took “will” throughout: the families who “will encamp” behind, south, and north (vv. 23, 29, 35), the prince who “will be” prince (vv. 24, 30, 32), the duty that “will be” each family's charge (vv. 25, 31, 36), and those who “will be” encamped before the tabernacle eastward (v. 38a). The same construction is flagged at four further places: 1:53b, where the verse's first clause took “must” (“the Levites must encamp”) but its second clause states a standing duty, “The Levites will be responsible for the Tabernacle of the Testimony”; 4:16, “The duty of Eleazar … will be the oil for the light”; 4:28, “Their duty will be under the hand of Ithamar” — those two noted by Audit Crew A-1 for uniformity, so that whatever global ruling Robert gives follows there as well; and 5:18, “The priest will have in his hand the water of bitterness that brings a curse,” the lone “will” inside a procedure whose every other step takes “must” (vv. 15–26). In all, 26 or more renderings stand flagged. Audit Crew A-1 neither overturned them on auditor authority nor blessed them as settled; the chapters pass with the flag carried, and the verses stand exactly as drafted until Robert rules. The question in one form is this: the whole arrangement is Yahweh's spoken camp order — is a prescribed arrangement a description, which takes “will,” as drafted, coherent with the descriptive line stated at Deuteronomy 28, or a prescription, which takes “must,” as the lane's Leviticus practice for prescribed arrangements and duties suggests? One further ruling cuts into the same question from the other side: at Numbers 8:25–26, Audit Crew B-1 accepted the drafter's “must” for the age-fifty Levite service rules — prescribed actions inside Yahweh's spoken service assignment, per Leviticus practice. Robert's single ruling on this item settles the uniform direction for both: either he confirms the descriptive “will” for this material, or he directs that the flagged duty and arrangement “will”s be converted to “must,” which is a mechanical change once ruled.

2. **Numbers 20:12, “you will not bring” — accepted as a declared outcome, carried PENDING ROBERT.** Yahweh's sentence on Moses and Aaron reads, “Because you didn't believe in me, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore you will not bring this assembly into the land which I have given them.” Audit Crew F-1 accepted the “will” rendering as a declared outcome and carried it PENDING ROBERT. The reasoning recorded in the audit is this: the sentence is declarative, carrying its cause and a “therefore”; it is addressed to Moses and Aaron jointly, about the journey's outcome; and the chapter's own parallel at verse 24 is outcome throughout — Aaron will be gathered to his people and will not enter the land — where “must not enter” could not stand for a dying Aaron. The verse is distinguished from the Deuteronomy bars at 31:2, 32:52, and 34:4, which are direct prohibitions of a single named movement laid on Moses (“must not go”); the lane renders each book's own form rather than harmonizing between them. The verse stands as drafted until Robert rules.

3. **Numbers 31:50, “ourselves” — PENDING ROBERT, expressly not a standing substitution.** The base reads that the officers “have brought an offering to Yahweh … to make atonement for our souls before Yahweh,” and the draft renders the closing words “to make atonement for ourselves.” Audit Crew H-1 did not fix the rendering under audit authority and did not approve it as settled: “ourselves” is not a standing lane substitution, and approving it would settle a cross-block consistency question that verse-fix authority does not reach. It stands provisionally as a disclosed one-off, stated twice in the chapter's apparatus. The options as framed in that audit record, for Robert's read-through, are three: keep “ourselves” as a one-off; render “our persons”; or keep the base's “our souls,” which the completed soul line's reflexive branch, stated in item 4 below, would support.

4. **The lane's three-branch soul line, and its Numbers instances.** The line the audits hold is this. Where “soul” stands for a countable person — a person who can be listed, tallied, or cut off — the lane renders “person” or “people”: in this book, at 5:6, at 9:13, at 15:28, 30, and 31, at 19:13, 20, and 22 (the person who touches a dead body or a grave and is cut off), and at 31:28. Where “soul” names the inner self or appetite, “soul” is kept as the base's word: at 21:4, where the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the journey, and at 21:5, where the people say “our soul loathes this disgusting food” — Audit Crew F-1 concurred in both keepings, the first naming the people's inner discouragement and the second appetite in first-person speech, with no countable person and no penalty in either place. Where “soul” stands in the reflexive idiom of binding or afflicting oneself, “soul” is likewise kept: at 29:7, “you must afflict your souls,” on the Leviticus 16 precedent, and in chapter 30's twelve occurrences of the vow-binding idiom, “bind his soul” (vv. 2, 4 twice, and 5–13), which Audit Crew G-1 accepted — legal precision is load-bearing in that chapter, and “bound her person” does not carry the idiom, while “bound herself” would rewrite every clause and break the word-for-word discipline. The rider recorded in chapter 30's audit record rides with this item: if Robert prefers the reflexive rendering (“bound herself” or “bound himself”), it must be applied to Leviticus 16, Numbers 29:7, and Numbers 30 together, so the line stays one line.

5. **Possibility and fact questions take “will” — confirmed, and outside the open deliberative-question item.** Three places in this book are confirmed on that footing. At 11:22, Moses' question “Will flocks and herds be slaughtered for them, to suffice them?” asks possibility and sufficiency, where “must” cannot stand; Audit Crew B-1 accepted the “will.” At 20:10, “Will we bring water out of this rock for you?” is confirmed by Audit Crew F-1 as a question of fact and possibility, of the same family as 11:22. At 23:8, Balaam's “How will I curse whom God has not cursed? How will I denounce whom Yahweh has not denounced?” is confirmed the same way. In none of the three is the speaker deliberating what he ought to do, so none of them belongs to the lane's open deliberative-“shall” item (Ruth 3:1; Joshua 7:8; Judges 13:12; Judges 20:18, 23, 28), and their “will” renderings do not wait on that item's ruling.

6. **Numbers 23:24 — a kept “Behold,” disclosed and flagged for Robert.** Verse 24 opens, in the base and in the draft alike, “Behold, a people rises up as a lioness.” This is the only unconverted “behold” in the Numbers drafts: everywhere else in the book the base's “Behold” or “behold” is rendered “See” or “see,” as each chapter's apparatus discloses. Audit Crew F-1 found the verse matching the base word-for-word, left the verse untouched, and corrected the apparatus to disclose the retention; the chapter passes with the retention disclosed. The flag rides for Robert on the Exodus 39:43 precedent, where a kept “behold” was likewise disclosed and the chapter audited PASS.

7. **The chapter 19 splits, and the word-order straightening at 19:5.** Audit Crew F-1 concurred in chapter 19's must/will splits. At verse 7, the priest's prescribed steps — washing his clothes and bathing his flesh in water — take “must,” while the resulting states — that he will be unclean until the evening, and afterward will be clean — take “will.” At verse 17, both the taking of the ashes and the pouring of running water on them in a vessel take “must,” as prescribed actions of the rite. At verse 20, the man in the case described is carried with “will” throughout, because the verse states a case and its outcome — being cut off from the assembly — and speaks no prohibition to him. Separately, at verse 5 the base's fronted list ending “…shall he burn” is rendered “he must burn,” a word-order straightening whose handling Audit Crew F-1 verified against the Leviticus 4:26 precedent, where the fronted line “All its fat … burn on the altar” is carried “All its fat he must burn on the altar”; the verse's punctuation is unchanged.

8. **Deuel and Reuel (1:14; 2:14) — base variation, not an error.** At 1:14 the base itself reads “Eliasaph the son of Deuel,” and at 2:14 the base itself reads “Eliasaph the son of Reuel.” Each chapter's draft follows its own chapter's base snapshot, and Audit Crew A-1 confirmed the difference as a variation in the base itself, recorded in both chapters' apparatuses and audit records. Neither draft is to be harmonized to the other.

9. **The descriptive-“will” line (the Deuteronomy 28 form), and its sustained instances in this book.** The line, as stated in full by Audit Crew M-1 at Deuteronomy 28 and held across the lane, is this: a descriptive or conditional “shall” — one that states what will be the case, in a census, a blessing, a curse, or a condition — takes “will”; “must” is reserved for a “shall” that itself does the commanding. Its sustained instances in Numbers, beyond the flagged arrangement material in item 1 above, include these. At 10:3–6, the trumpet blasts' outcomes take “will” — when both trumpets are blown, the congregation will gather, and when one is blown, the princes will gather — accepted by Audit Crew B-1 as outcomes following the blasts, against the arguable “must.” At 27:21, the verse's split is confirmed by Audit Crew G-1: Joshua “must stand” before Eleazar and Eleazar “must inquire” for him, as prescribed acts, while the outcomes of the inquiry — by whose word they will go out and come in — take “will.” And chapter 24's conversion is all “will”: the base's 20 “shall” occurrences became 20 “will” and 0 “must,” confirmed verse by verse by Audit Crew F-1 under the Deuteronomy 28 line — every one of the twenty states an outcome in Balaam's oracles or in Balak's parting words, and none of the twenty commands.

10. **Decreed death outcomes take “will”; the community's execution duty takes “must.”** Where a death is stated as the decreed outcome of a case, this book carries it with “will” — the stranger who comes near “will be put to death” (1:51; 3:10; 3:38) — under the precedent family at 18:7 and, in Leviticus, at 19:20, where the same distinction was verified against the files and sustained: a stated penalty outcome stands in “will,” while the duty laid on the community to carry out an execution — the stoning duty of the case law — stands in “must,” as a prescribed act. The splits in this book that rest on that distinction were accepted by their audit crews, and the distinction itself rides for Robert's read-through with this report.
