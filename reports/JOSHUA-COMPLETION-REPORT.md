## FLAGS CARRIED FOR ROBERT

1. **Joshua 6:9 — PENDING ROBERT.** The verse stands exactly as the base gives it: “The armed men went before the priests who blew the trumpets, and the ark went after them.” The current state is that this wording is the WEB Classic base's own, not a copying error at this desk. Audit Crew O-1 settled that point with its own live fetch of the base page during the audit: the live page reads the same words as the pinned snapshot and as the draft, so neither the snapshot nor the verse was corrupt, and both stand untouched. The controls read differently — the KJV has “the rereward came after the ark” and the ASV (1901) has “the rearward went after the ark” — and the base's own verse 13 reads “The rear guard came after Yahweh's ark,” which is why the verse 9 wording looks like a slip in the base itself. The base wording is kept, as this lane's base-faithful method requires, and an exception at this verse is Robert's ruling to make or to decline. The auditor's recommendation, carried with the flag, is to keep the base wording until Robert rules.

2. **Joshua 7:8 — deliberative “shall” rendered “will,” PENDING ROBERT.** Joshua's question “what shall I say” stands as drafted, “what will I say.” Audit Crew O-1 accepted the rendering at audit, with its reasoning on the record: a first-person deliberative “shall” asks what course to take and is not a command, so “must” would wrongly impose an obligation, and “will” is the least-wrong plain equivalent in the lane's settled scheme. The verse nevertheless rides inside the consolidated deliberative-question item that is pending Robert, because the same question stands open at Ruth 3:1, Judges 13:12, and Judges 20:18, 23, and 28, and the audit positions on record do not all agree: O-1's “will,” the precedent finding of Audit Crew U-1 that the audited Genesis and Exodus chapters keep the base's deliberative “shall” untouched, and Audit Crew S-1's proposal of “should” for questions that ask what ought to be done or said. One ruling from Robert will settle the class, and whichever treatment he picks will be applied back uniformly across all the places in a later fix pass under his word. No crew decides it here.

3. **Joshua 14:12 — “shall” → “will,” ACCEPTED, and judged outside the deliberative item.** Caleb's words “I shall drive them out, as Yahweh said” stand rendered “I will drive them out.” Audit Crew P-1 accepted the rendering and judged it outside the deliberative-question item in Flag 2, for a stated reason: this is a declarative statement of resolve, not a question, so the ruling on questions does not govern it. Both facts stand together — the acceptance is settled at audit, and the rendering is still flagged in the chapter's apparatus for Robert's read-through, where he can overturn it.

4. **Joshua 15:4 — border statement “shall” → “will,” ACCEPTED.** The base's “This shall be your south border” stands rendered “This will be your south border.” Audit Crew P-1 accepted it as a declaration of what the border is, not a command or a prescribed action laid on the hearers, and the treatment is consistent with the audited Numbers 34 border courses, where the border statements were likewise rendered “will.” The rendering is flagged in the chapter's apparatus for Robert's read-through.

5. **“Souls” → “people” — chapter 10 seven times, chapter 11 once.** In chapter 10 the base's “all the souls who were in it” is rendered “all the people who were in it” at verses 28, 30, 32, 35, and 39 once each and at verse 37 twice, seven instances in all; in chapter 11 the same rendering occurs once, at verse 11. Audit Crew P-1 confirmed every instance under the countable-person branch of the lane's soul line: in each place “souls” names a city's people as countable persons, each one struck and none left remaining, so the line renders “people.” Each instance is visible in its chapter's apparatus. For contrast within the same book, “soul” is kept at Joshua 22:5 and “souls” is kept at Joshua 23:14, where the word names the inner self alongside the heart rather than a countable person, and those keepings are likewise flagged in their apparatuses under the same line.

6. **Chapter 5's two flagged plain renderings, at 5:11 and 5:13.** At verse 13 the base's “he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man stood in front of him” is rendered with the lane's standing “behold” → “see,” keeping the base's word order, so the draft reads “looked, and see, a man stood in front of him.” Audit Crew O-1 accepted the rendering as the standing equivalent, disclosed in the apparatus, and carried the slightly stiff “looked, and see” as a flag for Robert, who may prefer a smoother order there. At verse 11 the base's “unleavened cakes” is rendered “cakes without yeast,” the lane's standing plain equivalent, with the word order adjusted so the phrase reads plainly; O-1 accepted it, and it too is flagged in the apparatus for the read-through. Both verses stand exactly as audited.

7. **Chapter 16's Yahweh count is zero — this is the base's own count, not an assembly error.** “Yahweh” occurs zero times in chapter 16, in the base and in the draft alike. Audit Crew P-1 confirmed the zero by its own exact count on the pinned snapshot for the chapter: no occurrence was dropped in drafting and none was introduced, and the chapter reads otherwise there, with no divine name in its verses. The zero in the reader's chapter 16 is therefore the verified count, stated here plainly so it is not mistaken for a verse lost in assembly.
