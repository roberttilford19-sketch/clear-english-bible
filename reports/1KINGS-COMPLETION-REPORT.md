## FLAGS CARRIED FOR ROBERT

1. **1 Kings 17:4, PENDING ROBERT — a flagged alternative on a confirmed rendering.** At 17:4 the verse reads, “You must drink from the brook.” Audit Crew AG-1 CONFIRMED that “must”: the word stands inside Yahweh's instruction speech to Elijah, opened by verse 3's imperatives, and drinking is a prescribed action — an act that is Elijah's own, contingent on his compliance, not a state that will be the case independently. Numbers 20:12 was distinguished on the record: that verse is a “therefore” consequence-sentence about a journey-long outcome, not a prescribed next act inside an instruction speech. The arguable “will” reading — “will” as a promise of provision — is genuinely arguable on the snapshot's English alone and STAYS FLAGGED here for Robert. The verse stands as audited, with “must,” and this flag carries the alternative; it does not reopen the audit's concurrence.

2. **The oracle line, closed by concurrence — 8:19 and 5:5, the same oracle.** At 8:19 Audit Crew AF-1 adjudicated the oracle's three “shall”s: Solomon quotes Yahweh's oracle to David, and those “shall”s declare the future course — David will not build, and the son who will come out of his body will build the house — so they take “will” under the lane's descriptive line, and “must” would recast prophecy as prescription. At 5:5 the SAME oracle stands quoted, and Audit Crew AE-1 concurred expressly: Solomon quotes Yahweh's spoken word to David, “will build the house for my name,” a promised outcome that lays no command on the hearer. The two places are therefore stated as settled-by-concurrence, not as open. The family's standing exception is noted here by cross-reference only, and it is not this book's flag: the oracle PROHIBITIONS at 1 Chronicles 22:8 and 28:3 (“You shall not build a house for my name”) were ruled by Audit Crew AP-1 to the same declared-future-course family, with “will” concurred at both places and the “must not” reading flagged PENDING ROBERT there, in that book.

3. **1 Kings 20:14, an AH-1 fact ruling — expressly OUTSIDE the deliberative item.** At 20:14 Ahab asks, “Who will begin the battle?” Audit Crew AH-1 ruled the place into the possibility/fact family, rendered “will”: Yahweh had already announced the deliverance at verse 13, Ahab weighs no alternative and decides nothing in the question, and his two questions form one informational pair about that announced event — by whom, and who will begin — which the prophet answers with designations, not counsel. The drafter's deliberative flag was withdrawn in the apparatus on that reasoning. The verse reads “will” under either classification, so no verse text turns on the ruling; what the ruling settles is the record the place belongs to, and 20:14 does not join the consolidated deliberative item.
