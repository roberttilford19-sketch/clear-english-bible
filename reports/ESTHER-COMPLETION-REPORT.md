No copyrighted modern translation was used as a base, a skeleton, or a check. This book is a clear-English modernization from that public-domain base. It is not a fresh translation made directly from the Hebrew or Greek. The draft was prepared with AI assistance. Robert Tilford is the editor.

2. **The “ordinance” scope flag — PENDING ROBERT.** The word “ordinance” is KEPT word-for-word at 2:8, 8:14, and 8:17. At each place the word names the king's published proclamation or decree: at 2:8 the king's published ordinance that gathered the young women to Susa, and at 8:14 and 8:17 the published counter-decree. The lane's audited “statutes / ordinances” → “rules” conversion was formed at places that all name Yahweh's laws; whether that conversion extends to royal proclamations is Robert's call, and the audits did not extend it on their own authority — at 2:8 “the king's rule” would misread as his reign, and at 8:14 and 8:17 “the rule was published” would misread the same way. All three places change together with Robert's word, or they stand as audited.

3. **Esther 5:1, the placement-class flag — PENDING ROBERT.** At 5:1 the king's words to Esther, “You shall not die” in the base, stand rendered “You will not die.” Audit Crew BE-1 ruled the verse to the assurance/outcome family: dying is not Esther's act, and the king decrees a NON-death with its ground stated (“for our command is openly declared to you”), on the decreed-death-outcome and 1 Kings 1:52 parallels. A dissent stands on the audit record and was NOT applied: that a royal “you shall not die” can be heard as a protective bar, and so kept nearer the prohibition family. The classification is Robert's to settle at his read-through; the verse stands as audited unless he rules otherwise.

4. **The 6:6 datum — on the deliberative record, PENDING ROBERT with the consolidated item.** At 6:6 the king asks Haman, in the base's own wording, “What should I do for the man whom I wish to honor?” The base's own word is “should,” not “shall,” so nothing was converted and the verse is kept exactly, with no “shall” converted at the place. Audit Crew BF-1 confirmed the verse as a datum for the consolidated deliberative item: it is deliberative in structure — the king is the decision-maker, the act is wholly in his own power, and he asks counsel on what he ought to do — and it belongs to the 2 Kings 20:9 datum family. Whatever treatment Robert rules for the consolidated deliberative item will be applied back to this place only under his word.

5. **Exclusions on the record — never to be swept into the deliberative item.** Five places stand ruled OFF the consolidated deliberative item, each with its auditor's concurrence, and they are recorded here so no later sweep gathers them in. At 3:13, the decree letter's base-own “should” (“When I had asked my counselors how this should be brought to pass…”) is the king's indirect report of his own earlier inquiry to his counselors, inside the quoted letter; Audit Crew BE-1 concurred in the exclusion. At 5:8, Esther's “the feast which I shall prepare for them,” rendered “will,” states a settled plan already announced at 5:4; it is no question and weighs no alternatives; Audit Crew BE-1 concurred in the exclusion. At 7:8, the king's base-own “Will you even assault my wife in my house?” is a rebuke of a settled act already seen, not a deliberation. At 8:6, Esther's “how could I see the affliction of my people, and how could I survive the destruction of my kindred?” keeps the base's own “could” forms; it is a rhetorical plea, with no act in her power weighed and no decision asked. At 9:4, the base-own “should” (“that he should be celebrated in all the kingdom”) is an indirect statement of the king's already-issued order, in a subordinate clause, not a deliberative question. Stated plainly at the point of the claim: **nothing in this book was placed ON the deliberative item as a converted “shall” member** — the book's only contact with the item is the 6:6 datum in Flags, item 4.
