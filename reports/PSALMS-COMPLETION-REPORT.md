## Method
Drafted from the World English Bible Classic base fetched live from ebible.org (public domain); KJV/ASV (1901) controls only; no copyrighted modern translation used as a base or skeleton. Every psalm was independently audited by a different crew than its drafter, verse-by-verse against the pinned WEB Classic base snapshot kept for each psalm: completeness and order, names, numbers, divine-name counts, banner and disclosure, plain-rendering fidelity, and no tradition-injected specifics. Parallels in other books were never imported: Psalm 53 was never harmonized toward Psalm 14, and Psalm 18 was never harmonized toward 2 Samuel 22, as Flags, item 6 states. This book is a clear-English modernization from that public-domain base. It is not a fresh translation made directly from the Hebrew or Greek. The draft was prepared with AI assistance. 2. **The reserved “soul” family — the Psalter's twelve members, all PENDING ROBERT, every verse standing exactly as audited.** The word “soul” or “souls” is kept exactly at twelve places that the audits placed on the reserved boundary family, where a group or collective referent stands under a survival, deliverance, or life predicate in the main clause, so that neither the countable-person branch nor the settled inner-self branch was ruled to govern: Psalm 33:19 (“to deliver their soul from death, to keep them alive in famine,” BR-1); Psalm 35:17 (“Rescue my soul from their destruction, my precious life from the lions,” BR-1); Psalm 72:13 (“He will save the souls of the needy,” BW-1 concurred); Psalm 72:14 (“He will redeem their soul from oppression and violence,” BW-1 concurred); Psalm 74:19 (“Don't deliver the soul of your dove to wild beasts,” BX-1); Psalm 78:50 (“He didn't spare their soul from death,” BX-1); Psalm 94:17 (“my soul would have soon lived in silence,” BZ-1); Psalm 97:10 (“He preserves the souls of his saints,” CA-1 concurred); Psalm 119:175 (“Let my soul live, that I may praise you,” CD-1); and Psalm 124:4, 124:5, and 124:7 (“the stream would have gone over our soul”; “the proud waters would have gone over our soul”; “Our soul has escaped like a bird out of the fowler's snare,” CE-1).
