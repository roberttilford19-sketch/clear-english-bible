## FLAGS CARRIED FOR ROBERT

1. **The song and the riddle stand as verse-paragraphs, not relineated.** Judges 5, the Song of Deborah, stands in the draft and in the reader as one verse-paragraph per verse, in base order and base wording, exactly as throughout the lane. 2. **Judges 21:21, the doubled “see” — PENDING ROBERT.** Verse 21 reads, in its opening words, “and see, and see, if the daughters of Shiloh come out.” The base reads “and see, and behold, if the daughters of Shiloh come out,” and applying the lane's disclosed “behold” → “see” rendering word-for-word gives the doubled “see” the draft carries exactly. Audit Crew T-1 ruled that the rendering stands as a faithful disclosed rendering, not an error: to substitute a different second word would add an interpretation the base does not mark and would break the word-for-word discipline apart from the disclosed rendering — the same ground on which the doubled “all my rules and all my rules” stood flagged at Leviticus 19:37. The flag therefore rides for Robert's read-through, as the chapter's apparatus carries it, and the chapter passes with it carried.

3. **The “see”-cluster stiffness flags at 3:25 and 7:13.** At Judges 3:25 the two “behold” renderings read “and see, he didn't open the doors of the upper room” and “and see, their lord had fallen down dead on the floor.” Audit Crew R-1 accepted both as disclosed plain equivalents, consistent with the lane's practice, and carried the drafter's flag PENDING ROBERT: the mild loss of smoothness is real, but it is a wording preference, not an error. At Judges 7:13 the three renderings in the dream report read “see, there was a man telling a dream,” and “See, I dreamed a dream; and see, a cake of barley bread tumbled.” Audit Crew R-1 accepted them on the same ground and carried that flag PENDING ROBERT on the same terms.

4. **The deliberative-“shall” item, as it touches this book — Judges 13:12 PENDING ROBERT, with Judges 20:18, 23, and 28 in the same places.** Judges 13:12 stands exactly as drafted: Manoah asks, “What must the child's way of life and mission be?” Audit Crew S-1 flagged the verse PENDING ROBERT and left the verse text untouched. Three audit positions are on record for a question's “shall” across the lane: keep the base's “shall,” as the audited Genesis and Exodus deliberative questions do (Genesis 30:31, Exodus 2:7, and Exodus 17:4 keep the base's deliberative “shall,” as the Ruth completion report records); render it “will,” as at Joshua 7:8 and as drafted at Ruth 3:1; and render it “should,” which is the single treatment Audit Crew S-1 proposed in the Judges 13 audit — “should” wherever the question asks what ought to be done or said, reserving “will” for questions of pure future fact and “must” for statements of requirement, not for questions. The consolidated item lives in the lane checkpoint and in Ruth's completion report, and this verse changes only on Robert's ruling. Audit Crew S-1 distinguished verse 5 of the same chapter: “No razor must come on his head” is accepted, because that “shall” is not a question but a stated prohibition laid on the child, and “must” is the lane's standing rendering for prescribed requirements and prohibitions. The coordinator adds Judges 20:18, 23, and 28 to the same item's places. Those are the congregation's questions to Yahweh, audited by Audit Crew T-1 as “will”: at 20:18, “Who will go up for us first to battle against the children of Benjamin?”; at 20:23, “Will I again draw near to battle against the children of Benjamin my brother?”; and at 20:28, “Will I yet again go out to battle against the children of Benjamin my brother, or will I cease?” If Robert rules one treatment for deliberative questions, these verses change with the same fix pass as 13:12. The base's own “should” is not part of the conversions and stands unchanged at 20:38 (“they should make a great cloud of smoke rise up”) and at 21:3 (“that there should be one tribe lacking in Israel today”).

5. **The “soul” line, with this book's three instances — 5:21, 10:16, and 16:16, all audit-accepted keeps.** The line the audits apply is this: convert “soul” only where “soul” names a countable person; keep “soul” where it names the inner self or life, or stands possessed as the seat of feeling. At 16:16, “his soul was troubled to death,” “soul” is possessed and is the subject of an inner state, naming Samson's inner self or life; Audit Crew T-1 accepted the keeping and resolved the drafter's unsure flag by that ruling rather than carrying it pending. The line itself rides for Robert's read-through, and any of the three keepings is his to overrule there.
