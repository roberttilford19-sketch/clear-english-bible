## §1 — What already exists

**The New Testament — complete, published, and in wide distribution (VERIFIED).**
*Di Jamiekan Nyuu Testiment* — the complete New Testament in Jamaican Creole, translated from the Greek, published by the **Bible Society of the West Indies in 2012**; launched in Britain in October 2012 and in Jamaica, in print and audio, in December 2012 (Wikipedia, "Jamaican Patois," Bible section, citing the edition; corroborated by the edition's own copyright page, §5 below). It uses the Cassidy writing system as adopted by the Jamaica Language Unit of the University of the West Indies. It is distributed today through YouVersion (version JNT), Bible.is, the Digital Bible Library (entry JAMBSW), and a Jamaican Creole New Testament app (Scripture Earth language page for `jam`, read live 2026-11-16).

**The Old Testament — a live, funded, portion-publishing project (VERIFIED as a project; its completion is NOT verified).**
The history matters because the gate's name has moved:

- BSWI's own Jamaican Creole Translation Project stated the aim from the start — "the Holy Scriptures (of the New Testament first and then of the Old Testament)" — with the OT "to be completed by 2018" (the project's own page, jamaicanbible.org, still carrying that line today; **the 2018 target was missed**, and as of September 2012 a BSWI coordinator recorded the OT had "not yet begun").
- The work that actually exists began in **2020** under **Wycliffe Bible Translators Caribbean**, as the **Western Caribbean Creole Old Testament Translation Project** — a cluster project covering Jamaican, Belizean Kriol, and San Andrés Islander Creole, born of a 2019 translation conference sponsored by The Seed Company, which came on board in October 2021 as the major financial partner, funding full-time mother-tongue translators in all three nations (Wycliffe Caribbean's own project page, hosted at spurropen.com; corroborated by Wycliffe Caribbean's site, wycliffecaribbean.org).
- Portions published so far, per Wycliffe Caribbean's own record and a first-person account by a Wycliffe Caribbean listening-group trainer (Woman Alive, Karen Roach): **Jonah, Ruth, and Esther published; Genesis translated** (account written after December 2023). The Jonah portion circulates as a Scripture app: "Text and audio: © 2023, Wycliffe Bible Translators Caribbean" (Google Play listing, updated 2026-02-04).
- On **2026-08-09/10**, at the Creole Pentateuch Celebration (First Baptist Church, La Loma, San Andrés), the **Pentateuch in Jamaican Creole — five of the OT's thirty-nine books — was dedicated and launched**, alongside the Belizean and San Andrés Pentateuchs; BSWI's Rev. Courtney Stewart preached at the dedication (the desk's 2026-11-14 status check, from contemporaneous reports and Wycliffe Caribbean's own programme; the two institutions are neighbours in this work, not strangers).
- A **complete Old Testament targeted for 2026** is stated in current reporting (Jamaica Untold Stories, 2026-10-01, attributing the plan to Wycliffe Bible Translators; Wycliffe Caribbean's project page states the aim — the project "will result in the publishing of the entire Bible in the Jamaican language" — without a date in the copy read). **Graded: a stated target, SUPPORTED; a completion, NOT FOUND.** Targets in this field have slipped before — BSWI's own 2018 target slipped by the better part of a decade — and that, too, is on the record.

## §2 — The gap, stated exactly (as of 2026-11-16)

Against the house's own canon — the Clear English Bible's 73 books — Jamaican stands:

- **New Testament: complete** (2012, BSWI). 27 of 27 books.
- **Old Testament: 5 of 39 books in print** (the Pentateuch, dedicated 2026-08-09/10), with Jonah, Ruth, and Esther published as portions before it and Genesis among the five. **34 of 39 OT books still have no published Jamaican form.**
- **Deuterocanonical books: 0 of 7.** The Wycliffe cluster project's stated aim is the 39-book Old Testament; the seven deuterocanonical books of Robert's canon stand outside that aim entirely.
- **Total: 41 of 73 books missing.** No catalogue lists a complete Jamaican Bible: find.bible / Digital Bible Library entry JAMBSW is NT-only; YouVersion lists JNT only; Joshua Project records "Complete Bible: None Reported"; Scripture Earth, read live today (2026-11-16), still lists the NT app and the single Jonah portion and nothing more (catalogue evidence itemised in the 2026-11-14 status check, re-verified at its freshest point today).

**The gate question the scout set — has their complete Bible landed? — is answered: NO, NOT FOUND after research, as of 2026-11-16.** By the scout's own letter, the Jamaican row therefore stays on the gap board. What has changed since the scout wrote is not the verdict but the *weather*: the gap is being closed from outside, in portions, by the community's own translators, on a stated 2026 target.

## §3 — The gate, corrected on the record

The commission named the West Indies Bible Society as "the institutional gate." The record shows the gate is **two institutions, and the living half is not BSWI**:

- **BSWI** holds the New Testament (publisher and copyright holder, §5), originated the translation project, and stands in the orthography's institutional line with the Jamaica Language Unit. It is the gate for anything touching the NT text or its tradition.
- **Wycliffe Bible Translators Caribbean** holds the Old Testament work — the only OT work there is — with The Seed Company as major funder and Jamaica's churches and the Jamaica Language Unit as partners.

No permission from either is required for the house to translate from its own master (§5), and none has been sought; this check contacted no one. But any honest launch serve must name the gate as it stands: a Jamaican pilot does not enter an empty field with one sleepy gatekeeper; it enters a field where **the community's own funded translators are mid-build and publishing**.

## §4 — What the house pilot would add — and the honest opposite case

**What it would add that does not already exist:**

1. **The seven deuterocanonical books.** No existing or announced project produces them in Jamaican. This is the one piece of the 41-book gap that duplicates nothing and collides with no one.
2. **The completion template itself.** Per the scout, the pilot's real product is the proven OT-completion template that at least ten later lanes will reuse. That value is real — but it attaches to the *template*, not to Jamaican in particular; Mapudungun (No. 2) was scouted precisely as the transfer proof, with no competing OT project found.
3. **Possible speed on the 34 missing books** — stated, and immediately qualified: the neighbour project is funded, staffed with full-time mother-tongue translators, and already publishing; a desk-built draft would still owe the native-review gate before it could be given as anything but a draft, so its honest speed advantage is unproven.

**The honest opposite case — where it would duplicate:**

1. **The 34 Protestant-canon OT books are exactly the cluster project's active brief** — same books, same language, same Cassidy/JLU orthography tradition, with Genesis through Deuteronomy already dedicated in print (2026-08) and the whole OT publicly targeted for 2026. A house build of those books is a second, parallel OT arriving beside — plausibly behind — the community's own.
2. **The scout's own retirement rule is written for this shape of fact.** "If their complete Bible has landed, Jamaican leaves the gap board and the pilot passes to Mapudungun." The completion has not landed, but the front's standing doctrine (Regional Reconciliation, collision watches) is that the front does not duplicate a finished gift; a gift visibly finishing, in portions, on a stated near target, is the watch case that doctrine exists for.
3. **The review gate cuts harder here than anywhere.** Jamaican has a settled orthography, a university language unit, and living translation institutions. A house draft in Jamaican that no Jamaican reviewer has passed would sit *below* the standard the lane's own community has already set — the honest DRAFT label protects the record, but it does not make the draft the lane's need.

## §5 — Rights and permission landscape (facts from the record; no legal advice)

- *Di Jamiekan Nyuu Testiment*: "**© 2012 by The Bible Society of the West Indies**" (the edition's own copyright page; corroborated by YouVersion's "© 2012 Bible Society of the West Indies" and the Digital Bible Society's "Jamaican Creole English 2012 Edition © 2012 Bible Society of the West Indies… printed with permission"). The NT text is BSWI's copyrighted work. The diglot edition's KJV text carries its own Crown rights notice.
- The Wycliffe OT portions are likewise their holders' work: the Jonah app states "Text and audio: © 2023, Wycliffe Bible Translators Caribbean."
- **The house's position, stated plainly:** the house builds from its own Clear English Bible master — Robert's own text — and does not copy, reproduce, or derive from BSWI's or Wycliffe's translations. No permission is needed to make an independent translation from one's own source text, and none has been sought or given. Two cautions ride on the record, stated as facts, not advice: (1) the scout's design uses the 2012 NT as a *control text* for orthography and terminology — reading a copyrighted translation as a spelling-and-terminology reference is a different act from reproducing its text, and any house build must keep that line bright, reproducing none of their wording; (2) the Cassidy/JLU orthography is a writing system maintained by the Jamaica Language Unit — a system of spelling, not a text — and the house's Jamaican Quran draft already stands in that same orthography tradition under the same discipline.
