Each position is carried in its own book's report.

## Decision 3 — 1 Peter 3:20, the boundary case

**The decision.** At 1 Peter 3:20 the base reads "eight souls, were saved through water," and the draft reads "eight people." Robert is asked to rule whether that conversion stands as drafted, or whether the base's word "souls" is restored at this verse.

**The places it governs.** 1 Peter 3:20 only. The ruling will be read beside Decision 4, because the auditor's dissent rests on the reserved family's places, and beside Decision 16, because the drafter's position rests in part on the Genesis 46 counts.

**What each choice does.** Letting the conversion stand leaves the verse reading "few, that is, eight people, were saved through water," on the counting ground. Restoring "souls" leaves the verse in the base's word, on the reserved pattern, and the verse then stands with the kept places in Decision 4. No other verse changes under either choice, because both crews state that this verse is the one place where the two grounds meet in a single clause.

**Where the crews differ.** The drafter's position (Draft Crew PE1-A, in the chapter 3 apparatus) is that the counting branch governs. The verse counts persons expressly, with a numeral ("eight"), a tally gloss ("few, that is"), and a located company (those in the ship while it was being built), and "souls" is the counting word for those persons. The drafter rests on the ship-company counting precedents, each audited: Acts 2:41 ("about three thousand souls" rendered "about three thousand people"), Acts 7:14 ("seventy-five souls" rendered "seventy-five people"), and Acts 27:37 ("we were two hundred seventy-six people on the ship," the base reading "souls"), with the Old Testament counts at Genesis 46 and Exodus 1:5 besides. The drafter tested the reserved question expressly and distinguished this verse on the ground that the saved are the counted persons in the ship, rescued through the water as persons, so the souls do not stand alone as the referent of a salvation predicated of the soul as such. The auditor's dissent (Audit Crew PE1-AUD, in chapter 3's audit record) is that the reserved pattern governs the classification of the instance. The auditor verified the counting ground in full and then recorded the distinguishing fact: in none of the three Acts counting places is an outcome predicated of the souls, while every audited reserved keep carries an outcome predicated of the soul itself, resting on Acts 3:23 (destruction predicated of the soul, kept), Hebrews 10:39 ("faith to the saving of the soul," kept), and James 1:21 ("able to save your souls," kept), with the James 5:20 pattern besides. At 3:20 both triggers stand in one clause, the souls are counted and a salvation outcome is predicated of them, and this book's own 1:9 keeps "souls" where salvation is predicated of them. On the Acts 3:23 logic, a tally does not by itself remove a predicated outcome. The auditor stated the drafter's distinction fairly in its own record and dissented from its governing force, not from its honesty. No fix was imposed, and the conversion stands as drafted while the question waits.
